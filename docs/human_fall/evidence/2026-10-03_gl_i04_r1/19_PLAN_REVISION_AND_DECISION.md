# Evidence-driven revisions and provisional adoption decision

The initial exact-witness strategy was safe on competition but exhausted 128 storage slots for low-noise single-plane hypotheses (06_experiment_run.txt). It failed the required positive usefulness check, not a reason to weaken that check. 07_diagnostic_tests.txt also exposed replay sampling after the frozen insufficient-FIT early return. Both original failures are retained. Production replay was corrected before final report/regression; initial vs final real stats independently match (14_audit_results.json).

Revised research design: every qualified raw hypothesis is refined before deciding retention. Exact duplicates can merge. Other witnesses can merge only into a fixed-anchor sufficient certificate: all normals lie in a spherical cap of radius <= half the protocol angular threshold, and total offset span <= protocol offset threshold. Sphere triangle inequality proves pairwise angle <= threshold; min/max bounds prove offset closeness. The anchor never drifts. Crossing either envelope bound creates a permanent unresolved reason, even if the surviving representative pair seems similar. Thus A~B~C cannot imply A~C. Independent offset and angular chain fixtures cover all orders. This is conservative certification, not a new equivalence relation or full clustering solution.

Storage candidate<=512 (default128), refinements<=2000, trace<=2000 and iterations<=2000 (defaults2000) are explicit. Every draw has a counted event; qualifying events partition into refined or unprocessed, refined into rejected/retained/exact-merged/certificate-merged/unstored. Any trace gap or unprocessed/unstored hypothesis remains unresolved. Runtime producer still limits seen draws to the frozen <=2000 sequence. The unbounded oracle lives only in this experiment script and covers only the same finite sequence.

30 synthetic comparisons: clean and 8mm noise each close 6/6 (3 seeds x original/permuted) and agree with the independent all-seen oracle. 35mm noise, 14cm double planes and 8cm close planes all stay unresolved 6/6; oracle also unresolved. Baseline remains valid for these high-noise synthetic planes, illustrating that testing all refined hypotheses is more conservative than the frozen retained-candidate policy, not proving a physical second surface. Late, duplicate, offset/angle chain, low-support distinct and every budget exhaustion are separate semantic fixtures, including terminal no-input and refinement rejection.

Measured preliminary medians (16_research_summary.json): clean/noisy prototype ~0.085/0.091s vs baseline ~0.037/0.038s; high-noise/dual/close ~0.126/0.124/0.151s vs ~0.041/0.048/0.048s. No speed improvement is established. Cost excludes expensive evidence oracle and is local Python3.12/NumPy1.26 only, not target performance. Peak retained clean/noisy1, difficult scenes128. Final experiment adds raw hypothesis audit fields and angular chain without changing acceptance thresholds.

Real approved input has no angle-qualified raw hypotheses; negative-X WHAT_IF refines693, retains31 witnesses and certificate-merges662, with 3519 distinct pairs in the all-seen oracle. Prototype remains unresolved (close competition/envelope gap), baseline remains competition_unresolved. These are geometric search witnesses, not proof of two physical grounds. Neither result authorizes a real candidate. All three posthoc holdout regions still exceed at least one frozen residual gate.

Decision pending independent OpenCode review: recommend using the diagnostic as an offline observation tool; **do not adopt the search prototype into runtime** in this item. It adds auditable completeness but costs >2x baseline here and is deliberately more conservative under high noise. Further algorithm work needs a bounded discrimination experiment comparing certificate-induced rejection against independent all-seen oracle on noisy single planes and ordered adversaries, counting close-to-best support and false-unresolved rates without changing 10deg/.05m gates. No new production integration is authorized here.
